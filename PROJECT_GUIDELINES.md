## I. Overview

The final assessment for this course will be an **open-topic group course project**. Students are free to form teams and independently select topics of interest related to data mining for exploration.

The final project will be evaluated through **TA Evaluation + Inter-Group Peer Evaluation (Voting)**. We plan to organize an in-class project showcase on **Tuesday afternoon of Week 12**, consisting of **Offline Demos + Online Voting**.

Detailed instructions regarding team formation, topic selection, and project presentations are provided below.

### 2.1. Team Formation Process

- The final course project is a **group project**, with **3 students per team** in principle. For teams of 4 students, the **final score will be multiplied by 75%**.
- Please finalize your team members and a preliminary project topic before the deadline (November 22), and register your information in the [AI3602 Data Mining 2026-2027 Fall Final Course Project Team Registration and Topic Selection](https://docs.qq.com/sheet/DWkJHT2pnUHVXVkpH?tab=BB08J2) document.


### 2.2. Suggested Project Topics
- The final course project adopts an **open-topic format**. In principle, any topic related to data mining may be selected.
- We encourage students to **build upon their preliminary projects**, extending them by incorporating cutting-edge techniques in data mining and artificial intelligence, or to refer to the suggested topic areas below.

Suggested topics include, but are not limited to:

- **High-Dimensional Data Mining.**
    1. Explore downstream applications based on the WebText dataset from the preliminary project (e.g., improving the mathematical reasoning capabilities of large language models).
    2. Explore other efficient algorithms for high-dimensional data, such as vectors and tensors.
- **Streaming Data Algorithms.**
    1. Improve and extend the detection of coordinated inauthentic accounts from the preliminary project (e.g., improving detection performance, incorporating social network information, and building a more comprehensive risk control system).
    2. Explore other algorithms or applications for streaming data.
- **Graph Data Mining.**
    1. Improve and extend the knowledge graph and Acemap search system from the preliminary project (e.g., enhancing search functionality, adding search intent recognition, developing a RAG-based research assistant, or building a DeepResearch Agent).
    2. Explore data mining tasks on other graph-structured data, including community detection, link prediction, node classification, etc.
- **Social Network Analysis.**
    1. Analyze data from social networks, such as rumor source tracing, social network analysis, etc.
- **Source Code Mining.**
    1. Explore data mining tasks on large-scale source code datasets, including traditional tasks such as code search, clone detection, and API recommendation.
    2. Alternatively, develop intelligent coding assistants for specific domains or application scenarios (e.g., automated code review and automated test case generation).
- **Security and Privacy Protection in Data Mining.**
    1. Investigate security and privacy issues in data mining, such as reconstruction attacks on user data, differential privacy, data privacy detection, and digital watermarking.
- **Recommendation Systems.**
    1. Applications include, but are not limited to, music recommendation, movie recommendation, and paper recommendation. Students may adopt relatively traditional funnel-based retrieval and re-ranking systems.
    2. Students are also encouraged to explore large language model-based recommendation systems or generative recommendation systems.
- **Spatiotemporal Data Mining.**
    1. Explore tasks involving temporal and spatial data, such as location prediction using vehicle trajectories and trajectory similarity learning.
- **Large Language Models and Data Mining.**
    1. Investigate applications of large language models or pretrained models in data mining (e.g., LLM-based content understanding, search, or recommendation).
    2. Alternatively, explore privacy and security issues related to large language models (e.g., detecting machine-generated content and identifying private information).

Project work may include, but is not limited to, building systems to solve specific problems on particular datasets using existing algorithms, or attempting to improve existing algorithms. During the presentation, please highlight the main contributions of your project, your reasoning and problem-solving process, and your analysis of the results.

The project showcase will consist of an **offline in-class showcase** and an **online project discussion**. The online discussion is expected to take place through **discussion threads on Canvas**.

### 3.1. In-Class Showcase Process

- The showcase is tentatively planned to follow a format similar to a Poster Session, although there will be no strict restrictions on the specific presentation format.
- Given the uncertainty regarding classroom and venue availability during the examination period, we will provide advance notice if the showcase format changes.

#### (1) Process Overview

- Each team will be assigned a designated location where they can set up their project presentation.
- During the in-class showcase, students may move around freely, interact with other teams, and attend their presentations.
- During the showcase, each team is required to participate in **inter-group peer evaluation**, and the evaluation results will contribute to the final project grade.

:::info
:information_source: Further arrangements regarding the in-class showcase and inter-group peer evaluation will be announced before the showcase.
:::

#### (2) Additional Notes for the In-Class Showcase

- Projects may be presented through **screen recordings, live interactive demos, posters**, or other formats.
    - If you plan to use screen recordings, demos, or slides, please bring your own laptop. If you plan to prepare a poster, please use A1-sized paper.
    - Posters of accepted papers are often publicly available on major academic conference websites for reference (e.g., [ICML 2025](https://icml.cc/virtual/2025/papers.html?filter=titles)).
- During the in-class showcase, **either Chinese or English may be used** for presentation materials and oral communication.
- Unlike a conventional presentation, the audience at the showcase will be constantly moving between teams. Therefore, please keep your explanations concise, emphasize key points, and engage your audience.

### 3.2. Online Discussion Process

The online discussion is expected to take place through a **Canvas discussion thread**, following the process below:

- On the day of the offline showcase, the TAs will publish a discussion thread on Canvas.
- Each team may **reply to the thread with an introduction to its project**.
- Students may **post replies** to discuss and exchange ideas with other teams.
- Students may also **like** the teams they support. Each student may like as many teams as they wish.

:::info
:information_source: Further arrangements regarding the online discussion will also be announced before the showcase.
:::

## IV. Grading

### 4.1. Grading Components

The course project will be evaluated through **TA Evaluation** + **Inter-Group Peer Evaluation**.

- TA Evaluation: During the in-class showcase, the TAs will attend each team's presentation and assign a score to each team's course project.
- Inter-Group Peer Evaluation: On the day of the in-class showcase, each team will also participate in peer evaluation by scoring the projects of other teams.

:::success
The **purpose of inter-group peer evaluation is to encourage students to actively communicate and interact with other teams**, while also preventing the evaluation from relying on a single perspective. The TA evaluation will carry a higher weight in the final grade calculation.
:::

### 4.2. Grading Criteria

| Criterion | Evaluation Standard | Weight (%) |
| ------------- | -------------------------------------------- | -------- |
| Topic Selection | Whether the selected topic is related to data mining | 5 |
| Workload / Completion | Whether sufficient work has been completed (including necessary experiments or the implementation of a functional system) | 15 |
| Problem and Innovation | Whether the problem scenario or proposed solution is innovative | 10 |
| Technical Correctness | Whether the methodology, implementation, and experimental setup are scientifically sound | 10 |
| Presentation Materials | Whether the presentation materials are well-structured, focused, and engaging | 20 |
| AI Usage Report | Whether the AI usage report is complete, detailed, and clearly explained | 10 |
| Oral Presentation | Whether the presentation is logically organized, fluent, and easy to understand | 20 |
| Inter-Group Peer Evaluation | Peer evaluation ranking | 10 |

## V. Submission Requirements

:::warning
- Submission deadline: December 6, 2026 (Sunday of Week 12).
:::

The presentation materials and source code used in the course project must be submitted for archiving.

- Please submit the following materials on Canvas before the deadline:
    1. **Presentation Materials.** Relevant documents and files used during the showcase, such as posters, slides, demos, and screen recordings.
    2. **Supporting Materials.** The project's source code.
    3. **AI Usage Report.** The report must address the following questions. For each question, if AI was used, please document your interactions with AI (e.g., prompts, skills, tool calls, returned outputs, etc.):
    - Which AI tools did you use in this course project? LLM APIs / AI agents;
    - When using AI to propose candidate project topics, how did you evaluate their value and feasibility?
    - How did you interact with AI to develop innovative ideas?
    - Did you use AI to assist in designing your technical approach, for example, by asking about specific code libraries or frameworks suitable for your project (such as RAG libraries or frontend development frameworks)?
    - When using AI to assist with coding, how did you debug and validate the generated code?
    - Which of the above steps did you complete without using AI, and why?
    - In addition to the above steps, describe any other ways you used AI, such as collecting, filtering, or automatically generating datasets; analyzing and evaluating results; or using large language models as evaluation metrics (LLM-as-a-Judge).
    - Across all tasks in this course (homework assignments, preliminary projects, and the final project), did you experience any "highlight moments" when using AI to solve problems? Your description may include, but is not limited to, the problem you aimed to solve, the AI techniques you applied, and how AI ultimately helped solve the problem. The more detailed, the better.
    - Summarize and reflect on your use of AI.

- A separate project report is **not required** for this final project. However, you may optionally include a brief explanatory document with your submission if necessary.
- Further instructions regarding the submission channel and required format will be announced later.
